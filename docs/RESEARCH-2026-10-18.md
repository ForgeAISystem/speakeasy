# What users want, and how to teach new features: research for SpeakEasy

Date: 2026-10-18. Scope: dictation apps, window and launcher tools, control surfaces, voice commands, and feature onboarding. Goal: decide what SpeakEasy builds next and how the Control Pad and voice commands get taught to an owner who gets lost when new features ship.

## 1. Method and sources

- **Hacker News comments, 2024 to Oct 2026.** Pulled through the public HN search API for "wispr flow", "superwhisper", "aqua voice", "dictation app", "macos voice control", "talon voice", "sequoia window tiling", "rectangle", "raycast window management", "restore window layout", "stream deck productivity" and "product tour". Each quote links to the comment. HN skews technical, so read it as the power-user view.
- **Forums and long-form reviews.** Mac Power Users forum (Stream Deck), Six Colors (a year with Stream Deck), TechCrunch, Digital Trends, Zack Proser, Wispr's own help docs.
- **Onboarding data.** Chameleon benchmark report 2025, Userpilot checklist benchmark 2024, Pendo feature adoption report 2019, NN/g tutorial study, Duolingo's own blog, Roediger and Karpicke 2006 (testing effect), Superhuman help docs.
- **Reddit could not be fetched** (blocked to our tools). Product Hunt comment threads did not surface either. Several review blogs are written by competitors (Voibe, Willow, Spokenly, Yaps, Laxis). We flag those as vendor sources and lean on them only when HN agrees.
- **Prior SpeakEasy work this builds on:** [WISPR-TEARDOWN-2026-10-12.md](./WISPR-TEARDOWN-2026-10-12.md) and [COMPETITOR-RESEARCH-2026-09-06.md](./COMPETITOR-RESEARCH-2026-09-06.md).

Limits: no controlled study compares learn-by-doing to video for desktop apps. Most onboarding numbers come from SaaS web products and from vendors who sell onboarding tools. Treat them as direction, not law.

## 2. What users want from dictation apps

### Top praise

- **Clean text, not raw text.** Wispr's edge is cleanup. Zack Proser reports 184 WPM and calls the gap "not close for my workflow" ([zackproser.com](https://zackproser.com/blog/wisprflow-vs-superwhisper)). Our own teardown found 23.3% of Wispr's final words differ from its raw recognizer, mostly verbal tics.
- **Accuracy on jargon.** Aqua users praise names and terms: "It gets everything, jargon, capitalization, everything." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46889253)). Another: "Aqua is in another league when it comes to accuracy." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43640084))
- **Custom vocabulary and replace rules.** "you can give it custom vocabulary and replace rules easily" ([HN, Superwhisper](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49749767)).
- **Learning from corrections.** "Especially learning the users nuances and corrections" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49746251)).
- **Voice as a first-draft tool.** "I believe dictation is the perfect first draft tool" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49746251)). Users say talking feeds AI tools richer context ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49353488)).
- **Zero learning.** Aqua is "bloody impressive" and needs no command learning ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43638007)).

### Top complaints

- **Price and subscriptions.** "I really don't get it why anyone would still pay for Wispr Flow" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50068571)). "I was not happy paying $12 per month" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49946389)). Superwhisper is "excellent but not cheap" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47669046)).
- **Privacy and cloud audio.** "I don't want my voice going to some company" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48577831)). Context features drew fire: "many of these dictations app opt you into Context awareness" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48531184)). Wispr's context feature caused a public controversy over window captures ([Laxis summary, vendor](https://www.laxis.com/de/blog/wispr-flow/)).
- **Quality drift.** Users suspect Wispr cut costs: "i think they are cost cutting on inference" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50052237)). One tester ranked Wispr "the least accurate of the 4" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49747122)). Wispr itself asked 700+ critics what was wrong; accuracy, reliability and a toolbar that covered buttons topped the list ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/wispr-flow-asked-its-haters-what-was-wrong-and-more-than-700-people-answered/)).
- **Latency breaks thought.** "If that loop has enough delay, I lose the thread" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48137064)).
- **Setup feels like a server install.** Superwhisper's most common complaint is configuration: models, modes, prompts, hotkeys, keys ([Laxis, vendor](https://www.laxis.com/blog/superwhisper/); [Voibe, vendor](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/superwhisper-review/)). One HN user sums up the field: "macwhisper is overkill, superwhisper too clever, and handy too buggy" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47042753)).
- **Heavy apps.** Users build their own because rivals are "super expensive or resource heavy" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49985020)). A competitor measured Wispr idling near 800 MB ([Willow, vendor](https://willowvoice.com/blog/super-whisper-vs-wispr-flow-comparison-reviews-and-alternatives)).
- **Phantom text in silence.** Whisper-family apps type words nobody said ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47990553)).
- **Hotkey clashes.** Willow's default Fn key clashed with Photoshop shortcuts ([G2 via search](https://www.g2.com/products/willow-voice/reviews)). Wispr's docs note Fn fails on external keyboards ([Wispr docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/customize/keyboards)).
- **Apple Dictation.** "apple's built in dictation is terrible" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47942572)). A minority defend it: "actually pretty dang good" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48195367)). Our own test put Apple's recognizer at 10.3% word errors versus Parakeet's 5.2%.

### Most requested

1. **Edit by voice.** "there's no way I can find to correct text" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47670423)). Aqua users loved live editing, but one found spoken edits "took a lot longer" than doing it by hand ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43641515)). Wispr's Command Mode answers this: select text, hold a key, say "make it friendlier" ([Wispr docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4816967992-how-to-use-command-mode)).
2. **Local and private by default**, with an optional own API key ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44945190)).
3. **Learn from my corrections** without a settings trip.
4. **Context and formatting per app.** Plain speech-to-text "lacks context awareness and formatting" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48896578)).
5. **Hooks into other tools.** "we just need good integration points" between push-to-talk and apps ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50044987)).
6. **One-time price or free.** Lifetime licenses and free local apps keep winning switchers.

What this means for SpeakEasy: we already match the top items (local Parakeet, cleanup, dictionary, app-aware style). The open gaps are edit-by-voice on a selection and learn-from-edits. Those are already ranked in the Wispr teardown.

## 3. What users want from window, launcher and control-surface tools

### What they love

- **Simple, reliable snapping beats power.** Rectangle "covers 80% of my needs and there are no pitfalls" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45555020)). Users who tried tiling managers went back: "it just felt kludgy and I went back to Rectangle" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38476147)).
- **Instant response.** Raycast window moves are "instant unlike the built-in alternatives on Tahoe" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47002903)).
- **One app instead of three.** Users moved window snapping into Raycast: "don't have to run a separate app now" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40627799)). This favors SpeakEasy folding pad, voice and dictation into one app.
- **No nagging.** Rectangle's free tier "doesn't nag me at all to upgrade" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40062739)).
- **One press does the whole job.** Jason Snell's Stream Deck rule: "Push a button, and the magic happens" ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2022/05/a-year-with-the-elgato-stream-deck/)).

### What they hate

- **macOS native tiling.** "window tiling shortcut requires dragging a window first" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41562433)). "impossible to disable resizing delay on tiling" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46217979)). Shortcuts "still don't work on non-Apple keyboards" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43440804)). Native snapping is "half-assed" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49734278)).
- **Layouts that fall apart.** Windows scatter after a monitor reconnects: "That's a super annoying macOS bug" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45385520)). Users want the system to "automatically save and restore window placement" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30881930)). The pitch that lands: "snap your windows, save the session, restore it tomorrow" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47474968)).
- **Stage Manager almost works.** "Stage Manager almost gets this right but then" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44278136)).
- **Raycast bloat and AI push.** "They're trying way too hard to solve non-existent problems" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44555048)). "Raycast is like Alfred, but with MORE AI." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47597845))

### Why people abandon control surfaces (Stream Deck lessons)

From the Mac Power Users thread "Stream Deck in Mothballs" ([forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/stream-deck-in-mothballs/44017)) and Six Colors:

- **They forget what is on it.** Snell built macros he "promptly forgot existed" until buttons put them in view ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2022/05/a-year-with-the-elgato-stream-deck/)). One user: "I made Stream Deck buttons for all three links. I never use them." ([forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/stream-deck-in-mothballs/44017))
- **Tasks are not frequent enough to form a habit.** "I don't do the same things on a regular enough basis" ([forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/stream-deck-in-mothballs/44017)).
- **Setup resets and breakage.** An update "assigned it new profiles, setting me back to square one." Macros built on screen-image matching broke every few months ([forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/stream-deck-in-mothballs/44017)).
- **The helper app had to be running.** One user quit the app on unplug and the buttons died ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46468444)).
- **Layers beat per-app pages.** Snell keeps a few context layers so "I'm never surprised at what I see there" ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2022/05/a-year-with-the-elgato-stream-deck/)).
- **Clear labels matter.** Snell ends up art-directing every button so he can recall it at a glance.

### Voice commands (Talon, Voice Control)

- **Strict syntax scares people.** Users want voice control "without me having to learn the talon voice syntax" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44339910)). Talon's own author says its "strict command system" confuses newcomers ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34847926)).
- **Natural language is the wish.** "I don't want something as finicky as macOS Voice Control" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50045019)). "simple one-word commands to system APIs don't work" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48467073)).
- **Those who push through love it.** "I was surprised how easy it was to learn." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44173663))

What this means for SpeakEasy: the pad is a Stream Deck without hardware, so it inherits Stream Deck's failure mode: people forget what the keys do. Voice commands inherit Voice Control's failure mode: people do not know what to say. Both are teaching problems, not engineering problems.

### What the examples teach

- **Superhuman: do it in front of me.** The 30-minute onboarding call showed a shortcut, then made the user do it live ([Gainsight](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/5-lessons-i-learned-from-superhumans-onboarding/)). In the app, hovering any icon shows its shortcut. After a mouse action, it nudges you toward the key next time ([Superhuman help](https://help.superhuman.com/article/478-superhuman-command)). Teaching lives where the work happens.
- **Linear: tasks, not a tour.** New users create an issue, open the command menu, set a priority. Each task surfaces one feature in context, and the checklist never blocks real work ([Supademo teardown](https://supademo.com/user-flow-examples/linear)).
- **Notion: the starter page is the lesson.** It asks how you will use it, then fills the workspace with matching starter content and a short next-steps list ([Supademo teardown](https://supademo.com/user-flow-examples/notion)).
- **Raycast: pick your hotkey, then use it.** Onboarding sets the global hotkey and walks through a search, a snippet, an extension and an AI chat ([Raycast manual](https://manual.raycast.com/quickstart)).
- **Arc: memorable and personal.** It skips import chores, runs a short colorful tutorial, and ends with a personal "membership card." It grew from the team onboarding users by hand ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/design/the-browser-company-arc-design-interview)). Critics disliked the forced sign-in ([TechYorker](https://techyorker.com/i-want-to-try-the-trendy-arc-browser-but-not-if-they-force-me-to-register-an-account/)).
- **Wispr: ask the goal, show only those steps.** New macOS onboarding asks dictation, meeting notes, or both, and hides the rest ([Wispr docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5926242953-product-aware-onboarding-choosing-dictation-notetaker-or-both-macos)).
- **Duolingo: value first, then small daily wins.** A lesson before sign-up. Streaks with freezes so one missed day does not wipe progress. Celebration on milestones ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)).
- **Games: the level is the tutorial.** Super Mario Bros. World 1-1 teaches jumping and enemies through layout, with no text, then repeats each lesson at higher stakes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_1-1)). Portal introduces one mechanic per room ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/intuitive-level-design-stop-teaching-start-showing)).

### What users say about tours

- "Don't build a product tour. It is useless friction." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48035264))
- "It's more frustrating that I can't make them go away for good." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48035505))
- Tours pushed after new features are "obnoxious" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48036284)).
- The one kind people ask for: "Some sort of wizard or interactive product tour to hand hold me" through the first key moment ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=8657833)).

### The pattern

Passive tours do not teach. Short, self-started practice that uses the real feature does. Retrieval beats re-reading. Small visible progress helps. Pop-ups get closed. The best teaching shows up at the moment of use and fades once the habit forms.

## 5. Ranked recommendations for SpeakEasy (top 12)

Effort: S = a day or less, M = a few days, L = a week or more. Impact is our estimate for a non-technical owner.

### 1. Pad Practice: a learn-by-doing walkthrough that checks real actions
**Build:** A "Practice" button on the Pad tab opens missions of 3 to 4 steps each. Mission 1: "Tap the mic key. Press A." SpeakEasy watches for the real pad press and the real window move, then shows a check. It opens two scratch windows to practice on so real work is untouched (Superhuman used a practice inbox). One mission per feature: snaps, workflows, apps and links, prompts, recording, voice.
**Evidence:** NN/g found read-only tutorials gave no gain and made tasks feel harder. Chameleon: 4 steps beat 5 (46% vs 23%), self-started tours finish about 2x more, launcher-started tours 67% vs 31%. Superhuman made users do the action live.
**Effort:** M. **Impact:** High. This is the core fix for "gets lost when features ship."

### 2. Hold-to-peek cheat sheet on the pad
**Build:** While the pad is open, every key shows a plain-word label ("Chrome left", "Morning setup"). Holding the mic key for half a second without pressing anything shows the full 4x4 map with labels and the voice phrase for each. Print view for a sticky note.
**Evidence:** Stream Deck owners forget what keys do; Snell relies on clear labels. Superhuman shows shortcuts on hover. Ubuntu's Super-key overlay. Users keep cheat sheets "in case you forget some" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21587128)).
**Effort:** S. **Impact:** High.

### 3. Start with a 4-key starter pad, unlock the rest
**Build:** Default pad shows 4 keys: left half, right half, full screen, one saved workflow. Other keys sit dimmed with "Unlock after Mission 2." Power users can turn on "Show all 16" in one click. Ask once at first launch: "What do you want the pad for? Windows / Apps and links / Prompts / Recording" and pre-fill only those keys (Wispr and Notion pattern).
**Evidence:** Pendo: 80% of features rarely used, 12% drive 80% of use. Superwhisper's dense settings are its top complaint. Snell: a few predictable layers beat many pages.
**Effort:** M. **Impact:** High.

### 4. "What's new" as one try-it card, never a wall
**Build:** After an update, a single small card in the SpeakEasy window (not a modal over work): one sentence, one 20-second "Try it" that launches the matching practice mission, and "Later." It never comes back after "Got it." One card per release, max.
**Evidence:** Nearly 40% of modals closed on sight. Embedded cards beat modals up to 1.5x. Text-only modals finish best (44% vs 21% with video). HN users hate tours that "can't make them go away for good."
**Effort:** S. **Impact:** High.

### 5. Teach the faster path at the moment of use
**Build:** When the owner drags a window to a half, uses the menu, or clicks a pad key with the mouse, show a quiet one-line hint for 3 seconds: "Next time: mic key, then A. Or say: Hey computer, Chrome left." Stop hinting a given action after it has been done 3 times the fast way.
**Evidence:** Superhuman nudges to the shortcut after every mouse action. Linear and Superhuman both teach inside the real work.
**Effort:** S to M. **Impact:** Medium to High.

### 6. Voice commands that forgive, and show what to say
**Build:** Keep the current "plan on the pad, then confirm" flow. Add: a "You can say" strip with 3 rotating examples tied to the owner's own apps and workflows; on a miss, show the closest match ("Did you mean: put Chrome on the left?") with one key to run it; never require exact wording.
**Evidence:** Users want voice control "without me having to learn the talon voice syntax." Voice Control is called "finicky." Talon's strict commands confuse newcomers.
**Effort:** S to M. **Impact:** High for voice adoption.

### 7. Save what is on screen now, and restore after monitor changes
**Build:** One pad action and one voice phrase: "Save this layout as Morning." It captures apps, windows, sizes and screens. When an external monitor reconnects, offer "Put Morning back?" as one key press.
**Evidence:** Window scatter after reconnect is "a super annoying macOS bug." Users want the system to "automatically save and restore window placement." "Save the session, restore it tomorrow" is the pitch that lands.
**Effort:** M. **Impact:** High for multi-screen days.

### 8. A speed budget, measured
**Build:** Targets: pad open under 100 ms, snap visible under 150 ms, no resize animation, dictation text on screen within 1 second of release for short clips. Log these in the existing stats and show a warning in Settings when a target slips.
**Evidence:** "If that loop has enough delay, I lose the thread." Sequoia's forced resize delay draws complaints. Raycast wins because moves are "instant."
**Effort:** S to set up, ongoing. **Impact:** Medium to High (felt quality).

### 9. Privacy you can see
**Build:** Local stays the default. Any cloud step (Claude fallback, Polished mode) shows a small cloud badge on the pad plan and in the indicator, every time. Any future "text near cursor" context ships off by default with a clear switch.
**Evidence:** "I don't want my voice going to some company." Wispr's context feature caused a public backlash. Superwhisper users drift into cloud modes "without noticing" ([Yaps, vendor](https://www.yaps.ai/blog/is-superwhisper-safe)).
**Effort:** S. **Impact:** Medium (trust; prevents a bad surprise).

### 10. Edit by voice on a selection, and learn from fixes
**Build:** Select text, hold fn, say "shorter" or "make it friendlier." Then quietly learn: when the owner fixes a word SpeakEasy typed, offer "Add 'Bella Vita' to Dictionary?" once.
**Evidence:** "there's no way I can find to correct text." Wispr built Command Mode for this. Users praise apps that learn corrections. Keep it fast: slow spoken edits lose to the keyboard ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43641515)).
**Effort:** M to L. **Impact:** High for dictation, already ranked in the Wispr teardown.

### 11. A light skill ladder and weekly recap, no guilt streaks
**Build:** A "Your pad skills" card: 6 levels, one per mission. A weekly recap: "You used 5 of 16 keys. You saved about 40 minutes. Try this one next." A 3-key challenge a few days after a mission ("Put Notes left and Chrome right in under 10 seconds") to lock the skill in. Use a weekly rhythm with a free skip, not a daily streak.
**Evidence:** Testing beats re-reading: 61% vs 40% recall after a week. Duolingo: streak holders 3.6x more likely to finish; freezes and celebrations help. A non-technical owner does not need daily pressure.
**Effort:** M. **Impact:** Medium.

### 12. A pad that never breaks or forgets
**Build:** Auto-backup the pad profile before every update, with one-click restore. Never depend on screen-image matching. If the helper is not running, say so on the pad. Detect hotkey clashes (Globe key, Photoshop-style Fn use, external keyboards) and offer a fix in plain words, like the existing Globe-key fix.
**Evidence:** Stream Deck profiles reset "setting me back to square one." Image-matched macros broke on updates. Buttons died when the helper quit. Fn and tiling shortcuts fail on non-Apple keyboards.
**Effort:** S to M. **Impact:** Medium (protects every other gain).

## 6. Walkthrough design principles we should follow

1. **Do, do not read.** Every step asks for one real action and checks it happened. No step is "click Next."
2. **Three or four steps per mission.** Never more than five. Split bigger features into more missions.
3. **The owner starts it.** Missions launch from a Practice button or a try-it card. Nothing pops over live work uninvited.
4. **Value in the first minute.** Mission 1 ends with a window snapped and a check mark, not a settings page.
5. **Practice on scratch windows.** Missions open throwaway windows so a mistake costs nothing.
6. **One idea per step, plain words.** Third-grade language. "Press A. Chrome jumps left." Show the key, the result, and the voice phrase.
7. **Show progress.** "Step 2 of 3" and a mission map. Progress bars lift completion about 12%.
8. **Teach where the work happens.** After the tour, hints appear at the moment of use and fade after the habit forms.
9. **Check again later.** A short challenge days after a mission turns a demo into a skill.
10. **Easy out, easy back.** Every mission has Skip and Resume. Dismissed cards never return. Missions stay replayable from the Pad tab.
11. **Celebrate small.** A quick check animation and "Skill unlocked." No guilt, no daily nag.
12. **Measure it.** Log mission starts, completions, the step where people quit, and whether the feature gets used a week later. Cut or fix any step where many people stop.
